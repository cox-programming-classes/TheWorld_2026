# The Presentation

*Fifteen minutes to teach us your idea, because we're all about to build on it.*

**On the fifth meeting, after four class periods of work.**  Each of you
presents for about fifteen minutes, questions included.  Bring your branch
open in Rider, and the game ready to run.

## Why there's a presentation

Every one of these four projects changes code the other three are standing on.
When you're finished, three people are going to open your branch and add to
it:  a new command, a new item, a new saved field, a new state.  They can only
do that if they understand what you built and why you built it that way.

So the presentation is the last part of the project.  You're the class's
expert on this one idea now, and this is where you teach it.

## What goes in the fifteen minutes

Six parts, in this order.  The times are a guide.

1. **The problem.**  About two minutes.  Run the game, or open the code, and
   show us what it couldn't do before you started.  Start from something we
   can see.
2. **The idea.**  About three minutes.  Name it, and say where it comes from.
   Teach us the two or three words we'll need, in your own words, with your
   source on the screen.
3. **Your design.**  About four minutes.  Show us the types you made and what
   each one knows.  Then pick one decision you made, and argue it:  what it
   cost, what it bought, and the alternative you threw out.
4. **Teach us to add one.**  About three minutes.  Live, in Rider, add one
   more of whatever your project makes:  a command, an item, a saved field, a
   state.  Narrate every file you touch.  This is the part we'll use most.
5. **Where it meets the others.**  About a minute.  Name one place your work
   touches another project, as a question for that person.
6. **Questions.**  About two minutes.  Any line on your branch is fair game.

## What I'm looking for

You don't need slides.  If you make some, keep them few:  the code and the
running game should carry most of it.

Your notes file is fair game in the questions.  If it says you decided
something, be ready to say why.

Sparky will rehearse with you.  Ask it to quiz you, or to play a classmate who
missed the explanation.  It won't write your talk.

## What good work looks like

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Getting Started** | You show the code and read it to us, and the problem it solves isn't clear yet. |
| **Got It Working** | We see the problem running, the idea gets a name and a definition in your own words, your design is on the screen, and the demo works. |
| **Made It Mine** | ...and you argue one trade-off, with the alternative you rejected.  You add one more, live, and we could follow you.  You answer questions about your own code from your own understanding. |
| **Went Beyond** | ...and you show us where the idea breaks, or where it came from:  the history of the pattern, or a case it handles badly in our game.  Or you hand each of the other three a question specific enough to start them working. |

**Made It Mine is the target.**
